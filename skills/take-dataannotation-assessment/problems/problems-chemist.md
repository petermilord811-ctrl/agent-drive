## Question 1 · Evaluating Reaction Mechanisms

**Type:** mcq + explanation  
**Title:** Question 1: Evaluating Reaction Mechanisms

### Prompt

Imagine a user asked the following request to an AI assistant:

> A chemist treats 5-bromohex-2-ene with sodium methoxide in methanol. What hydrocarbon product is formed and what is the general mechanism?

The following are three solutions that were returned by the AI:

**Response A**

I'll break down this reaction into steps:
High temperature favors elimination over substitution, and a double bond facilitates a second elimination, so this is a sequential two-step double elimination reaction.
The mechanism proceeds through a carbocation intermediate after methoxide abstracts the bromine, followed by deprotonation. This gives C₆H₁₀Br⁻ first:

C₆H₁₁Br + NaOMe → C₆H₁₀Br⁻ + H⁺ + NaOMe

A halide elimination occurs after the proton abstraction to produce C₆H₁₀:

C₆H₁₀Br⁻ + H⁺ + NaOMe → C₆H₁₀ + HBr + NaOMe

Thus, HBr is eliminated to yield hexa-2,4-diene as the hydrocarbon product. The final product is hexa-2,4-diene.

**Response B**

The product formed would be hexa-2,4-diene.
Reaction Mechanism: This reaction would be a dehydrohalogenation reaction, where a halogen bond is substituted by a double bond.
The methanol solvent first coordinates to the bromine, weakening the C–Br bond and allowing elimination to proceed more easily.
Reasoning: Hexa-2,4-diene would be favored over the alkyne form due to the superior stability of the localized electrons in the alkene product and the methanol driving the reaction.
Therefore, the hydrocarbon product formed by the reaction of 5-bromohex-2-ene and sodium methoxide in methanol is hexa-2,4-diene.

**Response C**

The reaction between 5-bromohex-2-ene and sodium methoxide in methanol proceeds through an E2 (second-order elimination) mechanism.
In this case, sodium methoxide acts as the strong base, performing a concerted elimination where it abstracts a β-hydrogen while bromide departs as a leaving group.
Thus,

C₆H₁₁Br + NaOMe → C₆H₁₀ + NaBr + CH₃OH

The conjugated (E,E)-hexa-2,4-diene is the preferred product due to orbital overlap in the conjugated π system. Na⁺ and Br⁻ are present as spectator ions in solution.

### Prompt

Next, you will read a chemistry problem and an AI's attempt to solve it. You will evaluate the AI's response and determine whether there are any inaccuracies or flaws in reasoning.

**User query:**

> A sample of cyanogen and its isomer isocyanogen are separately prepared and placed in an IR spectrometer. The samples are irradiated and the IR spectrum for each molecule is recorded. When the resulting spectra are analyzed, would you expect the spectra to have the same number of unique peaks or a different number?

### AI Response

Let’s break this down:

**Cyanogen and Isocyanogen Structure**

Cyanogen is a linear molecule with structure N≡C–C≡N, with a center of symmetry at the C–C bond.
Isocyanogen is also linear, with structure N≡C–N≡C.
Both molecules contain 4 atoms.

**Vibrational Mode Analysis**

For molecules, we calculate the number of vibrational modes using formulas based on their geometry.
Since both molecules are linear, we use the formula 3N−6 (where N is the number of atoms).
For both molecules: 3(4)−6 = 6 vibrational modes.

**Symmetry Effects**

Cyanogen (D∞h symmetry): Due to its center of symmetry, mutual exclusion applies. Some vibrational modes will be IR inactive.
Isocyanogen (C∞v symmetry): Lacks a center of symmetry, so its vibrational modes can all be IR active.

**Final Conclusion Based on this analysis:**

Both molecules have 6 fundamental vibrational modes.
Isocyanogen will show all of these modes in its IR spectrum.
Cyanogen will show fewer peaks due to its higher symmetry. Therefore, the spectra will have a different number of unique peaks, with isocyanogen showing more peaks than cyanogen.
